# Performance feedback

Feedback describes what happened and what it caused. It does not describe the person.

## When to use
- A manager needs to write or deliver feedback or a review summary.

## When to skip
- The matter involves a complaint, investigation or possible discipline. Escalate to the HR partner and legal counsel.

## Inputs needed
- The manager's notes in redacted form, with the role (not the name).
- Period covered and the standard or goal used.
- What the manager wants the person to do next.

## Steps
1. Sort the notes into observed behaviour (what was seen or done), impact (what it caused) and judgment (opinions).
2. Turn each judgment into a behaviour and an impact, or mark it UNSUPPORTED and ask for an example.
3. Write feedback in this shape: situation, behaviour, impact, what would be better, support offered.
4. Balance: include real strengths with specifics, not filler praise.
5. Bias check: flag personality labels, comparisons to other people, vague words such as attitude or fit, and uneven standards between similar roles.
6. Write two to three next steps that are specific, time-bound and owned.
7. Add three questions the manager should ask the person, to hear their side first.
8. If the notes suggest a ratings or pay decision, label the draft as input for a human decision only.

## Quality bar
- No fact beyond the notes. No rating suggested unless the manager gave the scale and evidence.

## Output format
Feedback draft, flagged wording table, next steps, and questions to ask.

## If input is missing
Ask for the notes and the standard used.

## Works with
Pairs with pii-redaction-before-ai and hr-ai-risk-review.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
