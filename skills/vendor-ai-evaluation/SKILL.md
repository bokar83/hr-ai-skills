# Vendor ai evaluation

Buy on evidence. Ask for it in writing and mark what the vendor did not show.

## When to use
- A team is choosing or renewing an HR or AI tool.

## When to skip
- The tool is already approved and the question is adoption. Use adoption-check-in.

## Inputs needed
- What the tool does and which HR process it touches.
- Regions of the employees affected.
- Must-have requirements, if any.

## Steps
1. Write questions in groups: data (what is collected, where stored, retention, deletion, training on customer data); model (what it does, how accuracy and bias were tested, by whom, with what evidence); security (certifications asked for, access controls, incident notice); sub-processors and locations; human oversight features; explainability; support and change notice; pricing and exit (data export, deletion at exit).
2. For each question add what a good answer looks like and what a weak answer looks like.
3. Add a 0 to 2 scoring column and a column for evidence seen (document, demo, none).
4. Flag deal-breakers the user names. Mark any legal or regional requirement: check current law and counsel.
5. Add a short list of references to ask the vendor for, and a pilot-first clause suggestion.
6. Summarize with: strong, weak, unproven, and the three questions to press hardest.

## Quality bar
- Every vendor claim is treated as unproven until evidence is shown.

## Output format
Question list, scoring sheet, deal-breaker flags and a press-hard list.

## If input is missing
Ask for the tool, the process and the regions.

## Works with
Pairs with hr-ai-risk-review and hr-ai-council.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
- Legal points are prompts for review, not advice: check current law and your counsel, not legal advice. Never cite a statute or article number you were not given.
