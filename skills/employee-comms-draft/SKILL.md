# Employee comms draft

People trust plain, honest messages. Say what changes, what does not, and what to do.

## When to use
- A change needs explaining to staff or managers.
- A rollout needs a FAQ and talking points.

## When to skip
- The message needs legal sign-off first and none has been given. Draft but mark it HOLD.

## Inputs needed
- The change, audience and region.
- Facts that are decided, and facts that are not.
- Where to ask questions.

## Steps
1. Write one-sentence headline of what changes and who it affects.
2. Write the body in short paragraphs: what is changing, why, what stays the same, what people need to do and by when, where to ask questions.
3. State honestly what is not decided yet. Never promise outcomes such as job security unless the user supplied that decision.
4. State plainly how AI is and is not used, and that a person reviews decisions that affect people (only if true and supplied).
5. Write five to eight FAQ items in the employees' likely words, including the worried ones.
6. Add manager talking points of five lines.
7. Translation check: avoid idioms, keep sentences short, flag terms that may not translate, and list the languages where a local reviewer should read it first.
8. Mark anything that needs consultation with employee representatives or legal review before release.

## Quality bar
- Reading level suits a general employee. No hype, no vague reassurance.

## Output format
Message, FAQ, manager talking points, translation flags, and a list of items to approve before sending.

## If input is missing
Ask for the change, the audience and what is decided.

## Works with
Pairs with hr-ai-risk-review and executive-brief.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
