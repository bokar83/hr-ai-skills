# Structured interview kit

Same questions, same scoring, written evidence. This reduces bias and improves decisions.

## When to use
- A role is open and interviews are being planned.

## When to skip
- The decision is already made. A kit built afterwards only justifies it.

## Inputs needed
- The role description or must-have skills.
- Interview stages and who interviews.
- Any skills that must be tested.

## Steps
1. List four to six competencies taken directly from the must-have skills.
2. For each, write two behaviour-based questions asking for a past situation, plus two follow-up probes (what was your part, what happened next, what would you change).
3. Add one job-related practical exercise if useful.
4. Write a 1 to 5 scoring guide for each competency with a plain description of weak, acceptable and strong answers.
5. Add rules: ask every candidate the same core questions, take notes on what was said, score before discussing, and compare to the guide, not to other candidates.
6. List questions never to ask: family plans, age, religion, health, origin and similar protected topics. Add: check current law and your counsel for your region.
7. Add a debrief template: scores, evidence, disagreements, decision, reason.

## Quality bar
- Every question ties to a stated requirement. No question probes protected characteristics.

## Output format
Interview guide, scorecard and debrief template.

## If input is missing
Ask for the role and the must-have skills.

## Works with
Follows job-description-rewrite.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
- Legal points are prompts for review, not advice: check current law and your counsel, not legal advice. Never cite a statute or article number you were not given.
