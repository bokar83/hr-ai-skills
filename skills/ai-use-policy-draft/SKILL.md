# Ai use policy draft

A short policy people can follow beats a long one nobody reads.

## When to use
- A team or company needs written rules for using AI at work.

## When to skip
- Legal has not been involved and the user plans to publish. Draft, but mark it HOLD for review.

## Inputs needed
- Approved tools (or UNKNOWN).
- Types of data in scope.
- Regions and any existing policies the user can supply.

## Steps
1. List what the user supplied and what is UNKNOWN. Do not invent approved tools or company rules.
2. Draft sections: purpose, who it applies to, approved tools, what may be entered, what must never be entered, human review rules, disclosure when AI helped, what to do if something goes wrong, who to ask.
3. Write the do and do-not list in short lines an employee can scan.
4. State that people remain responsible for the final work and any decision about a person needs human review.
5. Mark clauses that depend on local law or consultation: check current law and counsel.
6. End with open questions for the policy owner, and a review date field.
7. Offer a one-page version for staff.

## Quality bar
- No clause that the user did not supply or approve as a draft choice. Open items are visible.

## Output format
Policy draft, one-page staff version, open questions list.

## If input is missing
Ask for approved tools and data types in scope.

## Works with
Pairs with ai-incident-review and employee-comms-draft.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
- Legal points are prompts for review, not advice: check current law and your counsel, not legal advice. Never cite a statute or article number you were not given.
