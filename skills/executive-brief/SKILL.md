# Executive brief

Lead with the point. Leaders read the top and decide.

## When to use
- A long update needs to become a one-minute read.
- Meeting notes need to become decisions and actions.

## When to skip
- The reader needs the full analysis. Send it and add a brief on top.

## Inputs needed
- Mode: brief or actions.
- The source text or notes.
- The reader's role and the decision needed.

## Steps
1. BRIEF MODE: write a headline of eight words or fewer stating the point.
2. Add one sentence on why it matters now, then the ask: the decision, by whom, by when.
3. Add up to three bullets of evidence, each with its number and source, or UNKNOWN. Add the biggest risk and the cost of waiting.
4. State what is not known yet and when it will be.
5. ACTIONS MODE: list decisions made, each with the reason. List actions with an owner role, a due date or UNKNOWN, and the deliverable. List open questions and parked items.
6. Keep to 150 words for a brief. Cut jargon and filler. Keep numbers exact as given.

## Quality bar
- Nothing in the brief that is not in the source. Numbers are never rounded up or invented.

## Output format
The brief or the decisions and actions list, ready to paste.

## If input is missing
Ask for the mode, the source and the reader.

## Works with
Pairs with pressure-test and employee-comms-draft.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
