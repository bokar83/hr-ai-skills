# Discovery interview guide

Ask about real work, not opinions about AI. Treat what you hear as a hypothesis until it is checked.

## When to use
- Starting an AI adoption project with a team.
- Starting a new role and meeting the manager or sponsor.

## When to skip
- The facts are already documented and verified.

## Inputs needed
- Who will be interviewed (role) and why.
- What decision the interviews will inform.
- Time available.

## Steps
1. Set the goal of the conversation in one sentence.
2. Write opening lines that explain purpose and how notes are used, and invite people to say no to any question.
3. Write ten questions in four groups: what the work is (walk me through last week), where it hurts (what slows you down, what do you redo), how people use AI or other tools today, and what worries them.
4. Add three probes for vague answers: for example, can you give me an example, what happened next, who else is involved.
5. For a new role, add: what does success look like in 90 days, what is the unwritten rule, who should I meet, what should I not touch yet.
6. Add note rules: record their words, keep opinions separate from observations, no names in shared notes.
7. Add a closing question: what should I have asked that I did not.
8. End with a template to summarize several conversations into patterns.

## Quality bar
- Questions are open and neutral. None leads toward a wanted answer.

## Output format
Interview guide, probes, note template and a pattern summary template.

## If input is missing
Ask who is being interviewed and what decision it informs.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
