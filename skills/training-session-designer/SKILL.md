# Training session designer

People learn AI by using it on their own work, with clear safety rules. Design for practice, not slides.

## When to use
- An HR team or group needs to learn practical AI use.
- A pilot needs onboarding for its participants.

## When to skip
- The need is a policy briefing only. Use employee-comms-draft.

## Inputs needed
- Audience (roles, not names), their current experience, session length, tool in use.
- Two or three real tasks they do weekly.

## Steps
1. Set one outcome: by the end, each person can do X on their own task.
2. Plan blocks that fit the time: why and what not to paste (pii-redaction-before-ai), a live demo on a hypothetical task, hands-on practice on their own redacted tasks, checking outputs (ai-output-verifier), and what to do next.
3. Write two or three practice exercises with a starting prompt, a good-output description and a common failure to watch for.
4. Add a safety segment: allowed data, banned uses, who to ask. Mark company policy as UNKNOWN if not supplied.
5. Add a take-home: one task to try within a week and one question to bring back.
6. Plan a follow-up check-in two to four weeks later using adoption-check-in.
7. List what to prepare: hypothetical samples, access, a one-page rules sheet.

## Quality bar
- Every minute has a purpose. Exercises use hypothetical samples, never real employee records.

## Output format
A timed agenda, the exercises, the rules sheet text and the follow-up plan.

## If input is missing
Ask for the audience, the length and two typical tasks.

## Works with
Uses prompt-builder and adoption-check-in.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
