# Humanize: remove AI tells

Rewrite a draft so a careful reader would not suspect a machine wrote it. Meaning stays. Add nothing invented.

## When to use
- A draft has even rhythm, lists of three, stage directions or dramatic reveals.
- A piece will be read by many people.

## When to skip
- The user wants a voice rewrite in their own style (use a voice-matching skill or your own style guide first).

## Inputs needed
- The draft.

## Steps
1. Keep a copy of the original so you can compare.
2. Rhythm pass. Read aloud. No four or more consecutive sentences of about the same length. Include a very short sentence (under 6 words), a long one (over 25 words) and a single-sentence paragraph.
3. Remove throat-clearing openers: Let's dive in, It's worth noting, In today's world, When it comes to, The truth is. Start with the point.
4. Keep at most one list of exactly three per 300 words. Cut to two items or add a concrete example.
5. Keep at most one 'not X, but Y' sentence in the whole piece.
6. Rewrite colon reveals ('The best part: it learns') as plain statements.
7. Cut staged insight: What nobody tells you, Here's the thing, The part everyone misses. Open with the insight.
8. Replace puffed-up importance (a testament to, marks a pivotal moment, underscores the importance of) with the specific fact, or cut it.
9. Replace pairs like 'the confusion and the overwhelm' with a verb or a concrete phrase.
10. Cut closing recaps (In summary, To recap) or replace with one forward-looking line.
11. Vary bullet shapes, or turn a same-shape list into a short paragraph.
12. Replace dashes used as punctuation with a period, comma or colon.
13. Keep-or-cut test: every sentence that stays must add something the reader did not already have.

## Quality bar
- Meaning unchanged. No invented detail: a humanized lie is still a lie.

## Output format
1 The cleaned draft. 2 The habits found, with one quoted example each. 3 Any sentence that claims something with no support in the draft, flagged for the user to verify.

## If input is missing
Ask for the draft.

## Works with
Run after any voice or style pass, and before sharing the draft.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers or dates. Write UNKNOWN or [NEED FACT] instead.
- If you are not sure, say so plainly. Never guess to look confident.
- Plain English. No dashes used as punctuation breaks.
- Never send, post or act on anything. Produce drafts for the user to decide on.
