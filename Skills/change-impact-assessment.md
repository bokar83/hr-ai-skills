# Change impact assessment

Changes land on people, not on org charts. Name the groups and what changes for each.

## When to use
- A rollout is planned and the effects on roles and tasks are not yet mapped.

## When to skip
- The change is a single person's personal tool choice.

## Inputs needed
- The change in plain words.
- Groups or roles involved.
- Timing and what is already decided.

## Steps
1. List affected groups by role and approximate size (UNKNOWN if not given).
2. For each group state: what changes, what stays, what they must do differently, what support they need, and what they gain or lose.
3. Rate impact LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH with the reason.
4. List likely concerns in their own words, including fears about jobs or monitoring. Do not dismiss them.
5. Flag groups needing formal consultation or notice: check current law and counsel.
6. List the first three actions to take per HIGH group and who owns them.
7. Name what would tell you the change is landing badly, and when to check.

## Quality bar
- Every group impact traces to the input or is marked UNKNOWN.

## Output format
Impact table, concerns list, consultation flags and a first-actions plan.

## If input is missing
Ask for the change and the groups.

## Works with
Feeds employee-comms-draft and global-rollout-planner.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Say UNKNOWN (or [NEED FACT]) instead of guessing. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers, dates or sources.
- Draft only. Never send, post or act on anything. The user decides.
- Use roles, not names. Ask the user to remove personal detail before pasting.
- Plain English. No hype.
- Legal points are prompts for review, not advice: check current law and your counsel, not legal advice. Never cite a statute or article number you were not given.
