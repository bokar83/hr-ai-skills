# Lateral leverage

Do more by doing less with less. Before building or buying, ask what we already have that could do this job.

## When to use
- A plan calls for a new build, tool or purchase.
- A team is stuck or out of ideas.
- Any audit or project plan is being drafted.

## When to skip
- The outcome genuinely needs something new and reuse was already checked.

## Inputs needed
- The goal, asset or stuck point in one or two sentences.
- Optional: the new build or purchase already proposed, which becomes the thing to beat.

## Steps
1. State the desired outcome in one line, without any solution in it. Mark any proposed build or purchase separately as the thing to beat.
2. Inventory what already exists that touches the outcome: tools, licences, data, people, processes, idle capacity.
3. Generate 5 to 8 candidate moves using five lenses in order: repurpose an asset for a different job, recombine two or more assets, borrow a pattern from another industry, invert the problem, use a capability already paid for but idle.
4. Only after those are exhausted may a build-new or buy-new candidate enter, with a written reason why reuse cannot deliver.
5. Filter hard. Kill anything unethical or illegal first. Score the rest 1 to 5 on money impact, speed to results, feasibility this week, repeatability, reversibility and avoiding new builds.
6. Kill clever ideas with no plausible payoff. Rank survivors by outcome per unit of new stuff added.
7. Keep the top 1 to 3 only.

## Quality bar
- An unfiltered idea list is a failure. Something must be cut.
- 'Same thing, slightly better' is not a lateral move.
- Never more than 3 survivors. A new build survives only with a written reason.
- Ideas must fit the user's real situation, not generic brainstorming.

## Output format
Outcome and the thing to beat. Inventory (short list). For each survivor: the connection insight, the first action doable this week, and why it beats the obvious approach. Then one line listing what was cut and why.

## If input is missing
Ask one question: what is the goal or the thing we are stuck on, in one or two sentences?

## Works with
Run before what-must-be-true on any plan that needs a new build or purchase.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers or dates. Write UNKNOWN or [NEED FACT] instead.
- If you are not sure, say so plainly. Never guess to look confident.
- Plain English. No dashes used as punctuation breaks.
- Never send, post or act on anything. Produce drafts for the user to decide on.
