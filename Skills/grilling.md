# Grilling: pressure interview

Ask, do not tell. Find the weakest assumption by questioning, then stop and summarize.

## When to use
- The user wants to defend an idea before presenting it.
- A plan feels solid but has not been questioned.

## When to skip
- The user wants a written critique (use pressure-test).

## Inputs needed
- The idea, plan or position in a few sentences.
- Who will judge it.

## Steps
1. Restate the idea in one sentence and ask the user to confirm it.
2. Ask ONE question at a time. Wait for the answer. Never ask two questions in one message.
3. Order the questions: the claim (what exactly will happen), the evidence (how do you know), the alternative (what else explains it, what else could you do), the cost (what does it take and what does it risk), the failure (what would prove this wrong).
4. If an answer is vague, ask a narrower follow-up. 'It depends' earns the question 'On what, and which case applies here?'
5. If the user does not know, mark it UNKNOWN and move on. Do not supply an answer for them.
6. Stop after at most 10 questions or when the weakest assumption is clear.
7. Close with: the single weakest assumption, the evidence that would settle it, and the next step to get that evidence.

## Quality bar
- Tough but fair. No lecturing. No invented facts.

## Output format
Questions one at a time, then a closing summary of three lines.

## If input is missing
Ask for the idea and who will judge it.

## Works with
Follow with what-must-be-true or pressure-test on the improved plan.

## Rules for every skill
- Use only what the user gave. Do not invent facts, quotes, numbers or dates. Write UNKNOWN or [NEED FACT] instead.
- If you are not sure, say so plainly. Never guess to look confident.
- Plain English. No dashes used as punctuation breaks.
- Never send, post or act on anything. Produce drafts for the user to decide on.
